The payment formula

Monthly payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the principal, r the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12) and n the number of months. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.

How extra payments help

Every extra dollar goes straight to principal, which reduces the interest charged on every future month. On a 5-year, $25,000 loan at 7.5%, adding $100 a month pays it off 11 months sooner and saves about $1,000 in interest.

Affordability rule of thumb

Lenders look at debt-to-income: total monthly debt payments under roughly 36% of gross monthly income. On $55,300 a year ($4,608/month) that is about $1,660 a month across all debts.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good APR for a personal loan?

It depends on credit score and lender. Compare the APR (which includes fees) across at least three lenders before accepting.

Does paying extra reduce my monthly payment?

Usually not — it shortens the loan and cuts total interest. Some lenders will re-amortize on request.
