Why freelancers must charge 1.5–2× the employee hourly rate

Employers pay half of FICA, provide paid leave, equipment and benefits, and pay you for non-billable time. As a freelancer you pay 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of net earnings, fund your own health and retirement, and only get paid for billable hours.

Example: to replace a $55,300 salary with $6,000 of expenses, a 15% income-tax rate, 25 billable hours for 46 weeks and a 10% buffer, you need roughly $75–80 an hour — about 2.9× the $26.59 employee rate.

Pricing beyond hourly

Once you know your floor, move to project or value pricing. Clients buy outcomes, and a fixed price rewards you for getting faster with experience and AI tools.

Frequently asked questions

What is self-employment tax in 2026?

15.3% (12.4% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base plus 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net self-employment earnings. Half is deductible for income tax.

How many billable hours are realistic?

20–30 per week for most solo professionals once you account for sales, admin and learning.
